I bet you were expecting to see a post here about installing the ceiling next. Well, I got a bit distracted.

A while back, my wife mentioned how bad the factory backup camera and the after market backup camera were. The radio also looked like it was straight out of 1995.

![Original factory radio](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0288.jpg)

Therefore, I had the idea to replace the back-up camera and radio in the back of my mind as a future project while I was working on the ceiling. Then, while searching for van ceiling videos, I found a video of a guy installing a 360-degree camera system and a 10.5-inch radio, a Dudu 7, on his 2025 Transit van. Yes, that is the brand name. Yes, it is hilarious to my 10-year-old.

I got sucked in, and the next thing I knew, I had ordered one.

![New Dudu 7 radio ready to install](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0314.jpg)

Hey, even if I'm not working, every once in a while you have to give yourself a work break, right?

So I dove right in and started pulling the old radio out.

![Pulling out the factory radio](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0319.jpg)

One of the nice things about the product I ordered is that they make the front casing custom for each vehicle, so fitting the new radio into the existing panel was a breeze. Additionally, their support (as well as the re-seller on Amazon) is phenomenal. I had several detailed questions before I purchased the unit, including how it would hook into my factory Ford system. They answered very promptly and gave me exactly the information I needed to proceed. 

![New radio with custom face plate to fit the Transit panel](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0320.jpg)

Also, after my recent experience relocating the van wire loom, large quantities of OEM Transit wires did not deter me.

![Factory wiring behind the radio](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0323.jpg)

The old radio was deep in the panel, but just like the rest of the frame, it came out easily with only a few 7mm bolts.

![Factory radio removed from the panel](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0325.jpg)

Before I knew it, the new radio was plugged in and working.

![New radio powered on and working](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0326.jpg)

This looks like a mess, but keeping everything out while testing saved me some time. Once I had everything working, I left the 360-degree camera cables loose so I could start dry fitting the four cameras that support the 360-degree functionality.

![Camera wiring left loose for testing](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0327.jpg)

I was not sure I could do it at first, but thanks to my wire loom experience, I was able to remove the factory rear-view camera and replace it in the original upper rear light housing.

![New Rearview camera installed in the original housing](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0328.jpg)

The side cameras and front camera were taped into position for testing.

![Side camera taped into position](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0329.jpg)

![Front camera taped into position](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0330.jpg)

Once I was satisfied with the locations, I drilled the final holes for the side cameras, and they ended up looking pretty good.

![Final side camera location drilled from the inside wall panel](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0335.jpg)

![Side camera installed](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0336.jpg)

The front camera was even easier to install. It simply attached to the front bumper with screws.

![Front camera installed on the bumper](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0337.jpg)

With the physical installation complete, I started customizing all the different ways the radio can be used. It has an awesome default three-panel window system that is completely customizable and allows up to 16 different screens.

The default screen I have set up shows the map, music, a compass, and a vehicle representation with the speedometer, average speed, and door status.

![Custom three-panel radio layout](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0368.jpg)

One of my favorite customizations is an auto-leveling widget, shown here in the bottom middle.

![Auto-leveling widget on the new radio](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0367.jpg)

I have not calibrated the 360-degree camera functionality yet, but even using the four cameras in their default view, the picture quality and options are amazing.

![Rear view and uncalibrated 360-degree camera view](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0361.jpg)

You can even use picture-in-picture to look at different camera views while still showing the main screen.

![Picture-in-picture camera view](/images/NewRadio/IMG_0369.jpg)

This was a fun diversion, and once I get the cameras calibrated, it should be a very powerful tool for parking, backing up, and navigating the narrow gravel roads we eventually want to access.
