One of the big Phase 2 projects we had been planning for a long time was installing rear capsules. The goal was simple enough: widen the sleeping area so I could finally sleep full length, raise the bed, and make the rest of the interior build work better. The execution was a little less simple, because it involved cutting two very large holes in the van.

Before anything went near the van, we started by Raptor Lining the capsules. This gave them a rough, durable, black textured surface that should hold up well over time.

![Capsule before Raptor Liner](/images/Capsules/IMG_4622.jpg)

![Raptor Liner applied to the capsules](/images/Capsules/IMG_4623.jpg)

![Final Raptor Liner texture](/images/Capsules/IMG_4628.jpg)

This took several coats. Once the capsules were dry, it was time to prep the van. We taped the outside and inside so metal particles from cutting would not stick all over the van and potentially create future rust problems.

![Taping the outside of the van before cutting](/images/Capsules/IMG_0003.jpg)

![Taping inside the van to catch metal particles](/images/Capsules/IMG_0004.jpg)

We also taped around the outside of the panel where the cut would happen. The idea was that once the panel was cut free, it would not immediately fall and tear up any of the edges.

![Taped cut area on the outside of the van](/images/Capsules/IMG_0005.jpg)

Before cutting this large of a hole in the van, we watched endless videos to make sure we were not making any obvious mistakes. There are no do-overs with this process. The actual cutting went pretty smoothly though. We cut from inside the van with a jigsaw fitted with a fine metal cutting blade, using the van itself as the template. Inside the van, the single-sided panels show the contour where a window would go, which gave us the shape to follow.

Once the first cut was done, we were excited and scared. Excited because it had worked. Scared because, well, there was now a giant hole in the van.

![The first large cut completed](/images/Capsules/IMG_0006.jpg)

![Looking through the new opening](/images/Capsules/IMG_0007.jpg)

The next question was the important one: would the capsules actually fit?

Before finding out, we cleaned up the raw edge. We deburred the cut metal and painted the exposed edge with anti-rust paint.

![Deburring and painting the edge](/images/Capsules/IMG_0009.jpg)

Then we applied Sikaflex to the capsules. Sikaflex is a heavy silicone sealant used for applications like adhering car windshields, so it was the right kind of serious adhesive for this job. After that, we clamped the capsules to the van and waited.

![Capsules clamped to the van](/images/Capsules/IMG_0011.jpg)

Fortunately, everything fit.

![Capsule installed and fitting properly](/images/Capsules/IMG_0013.jpg)

Once the capsules were installed, we added the windows. Then, 48 hours later, we did the water test. No leaks!

![Window installed after the leak test](/images/Capsules/IMG_0014.jpg)

I am very glad to have this project done. It was one of the more intimidating parts of the build, but it unlocked the next phase of the interior. Now we can rearrange things, raise the bed, and finish the rest of the inside of the van.

And as a side benefit, once we got the bed back in the van, the capsules finally allowed me to sleep full length. That meant a good night's sleep, which is a very nice reward for cutting giant holes in a perfectly good van.
