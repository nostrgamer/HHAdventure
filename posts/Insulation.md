One of the components my wife had been waiting for over a year to have in the van was insulation. This was a much-needed upgrade from the big temperature swings and that baked (or frozen) feeling of being inside an uninsulated tent or vehicle.

For the insulation, we chose Havelock Wool and picked it up from their location in Reno, Nevada.

![Havelock Wool insulation with my son's van art](/images/Insulation/IMG_0073.jpg)

We started by filling the doors and walls with insulation.

![Adding insulation into the van door cavities](/images/Insulation/IMG_0084.jpg)

![Insulation tucked into the wall sections](/images/Insulation/IMG_0085.jpg)

For the ceiling, we used 3M adhesive to temporarily hold the wool in place.

![Using adhesive for the ceiling insulation](/images/Insulation/IMG_0068.jpg)

The work moved pretty quickly once we got started.

![Insulation progress inside the van](/images/Insulation/IMG_0065.jpg)

![More insulation installed](/images/Insulation/IMG_0064.jpg)

Soon the van started to look and feel much more filled in.

![Van filling up with wool insulation](/images/Insulation/IMG_0069.jpg)

![Insulation installed across more of the interior](/images/Insulation/IMG_0074.jpg)

My son was a great help along the way and enjoyed both the installation process and the final product.

![Enjoying the newly insulated van](/images/Insulation/IMG_0072.jpg)

The van already feels more temperate, which is exactly what we were hoping for. As an added bonus, the wool insulation should also help mitigate some of the moisture and condensation problems that small spaces can have when occupied by sleeping people.

This is one of those upgrades that is mostly hidden once the build is finished, but it should make a big difference every time we use the van.
