After months of having the lights and insulation dangling in our faces, I was anxious to finally get the ceiling up. Before I could put the ceiling in place, though, I realized there was one more inconvenient problem hiding at the back of the van.

The back wall where the ceiling meets the rear of the van was covered by a plastic cover, and the factory wire loom ran underneath it and then along the wall.

![Original wire loom location, cover visable at the rear B-pillar](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0168.jpg)

That meant the cover had to come off and the loom had to be removed from its original path. 

![Cover removed from the loom on the rear B-pillar](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0264.jpg)

To start that process, I had to find the end of the wire. At first, I thought it terminated in the bumper.

![Looking for the wire termination in the rear bumper](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0268.jpg)

It turns out the actual termination was in the side panel on the passenger side, near the rear door magnet. I only figured that out after spending a few hours poking around in a number of crooks and crannies at the back of the van.

![Searching through the rear of the van for the wire path](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0267.jpg)

![The actual wire termination near the rear door magnet](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0272.jpg)

So, naturally, I spent several hours disassembling the rear bumper for no reason.

![Rear bumper, tail lights, and associated parts, disassembled](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0278.jpg)

The good news is that I learned a lot about how the rear sensors and wiring system work. That ended up being useful later when I replaced the rear backup camera, so at least the detour paid me back eventually.

Once the wire loom was finally freed from the back of the van, I had a lovely pile of wires that I was going to have to somehow put back together.

![Freed wire loom at the back of the van](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0270.jpg)

![A pile of wires ready to be sorted and reconnected](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0271.jpg)

Fortunately, I had already learned one important trick while wiring up the house battery: the miracle of FrogTape and a Sharpie.

![Labeling the wiring with tape and marker](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0275.jpg)

Eventually, I was able to get the wires out of the rear pillar and off the side wall entirely, which created a glorious mess of wire for a little while.

![Wire harness pulled out of the rear pillar and side wall](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0279.jpg)

In the foreground of that shot, you can also see a bunch of wire wrapped in blue electrical tape. Way too much wire, due to a poor install job. This had been installed as part of a "new" backup and side camera system that was terrible and perhaps from the early 2000s (yet the van is a 2020 van). It attached to a processor unit that was thick and ate up a ton of space on the driver-side wall, which would prevent easily installation of my walls in the future.

![Old camera-system wiring taking up space on the wall](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0265.jpg)

Fortunately, all of that old wiring could be removed, and the wall became much tidier.

![Old camera wiring removed from the wall](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0282.jpg)

Finally, I brought the factory loom up to the ceiling line on the wall and fed it through the back B-pillar. This should make both the ceiling install and the future wall installation much easier.

![Wire loom moved up toward the ceiling](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0283.jpg)

![Wire loom fed through the rear pillar](/images/Wire%20Harness/IMG_0286.jpg)

This was one of those jobs that looked like a mess in the middle and did not make the van feel more finished right away. But it cleared a major obstacle before the ceiling and walls could go in, and it gave me a much better understanding of the rear wiring before the camera work which would come later.
