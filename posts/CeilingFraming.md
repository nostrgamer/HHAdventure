With the wire loom now out of the way, it was time to start putting up the framing that would support the ceiling.

A lot of van builders use shiplap wood for the ceiling, but we wanted something lighter, so we are planning to build ours out of plywood. The plywood still needs something solid to attach to, which meant my next job was framing the ceiling of the van.

I started at the front by placing a three-quarter-inch piece across the metal frame where the blobs used to sit, along with another framing piece on the foremost rib of the van.

![Front ceiling framing where the blobs used to sit](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0166.jpg)

![Framing piece attached to the foremost ceiling rib](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0298.jpg)

"Blobs" is our unofficial term for the foam pieces that cover the airbags. In the previous photos, you can see the green airbag on the driver side just below the headliner. I had previously removed the blobs so I could take down the headliner, install insulation behind it, and add our new headliner shelf. The blobs will eventually be trimmed and reinstalled where they meet the new ceiling and wall pieces.

The remaining ceiling framing pieces did not have anything convenient to attach to without modification. That meant every ceiling rib needed rivnuts. I drilled new holes, working from below, then punched, sanded, painted, and pressed the rivnuts into place.

![New rivnut holes drilled in the ceiling ribs](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0167.jpg)

This picture does not look like much, but installing 64 rivnuts took a lot of time.

Next, I moved to the back of the van and installed a cross piece for the ceiling panels to screw into.

![Rear cross piece installed for the ceiling](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0290.jpg)

Then the lengthwise framing went up using the rivnut locations I had just installed.

![Lengthwise ceiling framing installed](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0289.jpg)

The rivnuts and lengthwise framing had to be placed 12.5 inches from the join of the ceiling and wall, with an additional quarter-inch gap to account for the final wall panels. That spacing should allow us to install the soft upper shelving we have already selected.

At the rear of the van, there was no ceiling rib to attach to, so the lengthwise piece was Kreg-jig screwed into the rear framing piece.

![Rear lengthwise framing attached with pocket screws](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0293.jpg)

Next, I installed widthwise framing pieces in the middle of the van to give the ceiling more hardpoints to attach to.

![Widthwise framing pieces added across the van ribs](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0294.jpg)

I also added more framing as close as possible to the join between the wall and ceiling for additional structural support.

![Additional framing near the wall and ceiling join](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0299.jpg)

Originally, when I framed the upper walls, I did not have the exact dimensions of the upper cabinets we plan to use.

![Original upper wall framing before cabinet spacing adjustment](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0302.jpg)

Once I had the cabinet dimensions, those pieces had to be unfastened and re-drilled slightly higher to accommodate the 12.5-inch cabinet height plus the quarter-inch gap for the soft cabinets we want to install.

![Upper wall framing adjusted for the future cabinets](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0303.jpg)

Finally, I installed framing pieces along the wall as close to the ceiling as possible. That required even more drilling for rivnuts. 

![Passenger-side upper wall framing installed](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0310.jpg)

On the driver side, I repeated the same process, except near the blob location where a special cut was needed to accommodate reinstallation later.

![Driver-side framing with a special cut for the blob](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0311.jpg)

This was another unglamorous job that took a lot of time and does not look like much was accomplished.

![Ceiling framing completed and ready for panels](/images/CeilingFraming/IMG_0313.jpg)

Still, with this step complete, we were finally ready to install ceiling panels, much to my relief, and my shoulders' relief.
