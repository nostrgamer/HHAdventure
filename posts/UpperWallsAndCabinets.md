With the ceiling finished, our next goal was to complete the upper walls and install the cabinets before our next camping trip, which was only a week or so away.

Fortunately, the ceiling work had helped us develop a much more efficient method for fitting and finishing the plywood panels. Even better, the walls did not require nearly as much overhead work.

Before we started cutting panels, though, we needed to figure out how the ceiling and upper walls would meet without leaving visible gaps. Jenn found a rubber gasket with adhesive on one side that would cover the joint neatly. She measured the gaps first to make sure the gasket would provide enough coverage.

![Jenn measuring the ceiling and wall gap for the rubber gasket](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0389.jpg)

At the front of the van, I also needed to trim the foam "blobs" so we could determine where the upper wall panels would terminate. The blobs will be trimmed further and covered with foam and fabric during a later stage of the build.

![Trimming a foam blob to establish the end of the upper wall panel](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0390.jpg)

With those details sorted out, we started dry-fitting the quarter-inch Baltic birch wall panels, beginning at the rear of the van and then moving toward the front.

![Dry-fitting the rear upper wall panel](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0443.jpg)

![Dry-fitting the front upper wall panel](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0444.jpg)

Once we were happy with the fit, we removed the panels and followed the same finishing process we had used for the ceiling: sanding, cleaning, and applying several coats of clear finish.

The passenger-side panel needed an opening for our dimmable light switch.

![Passenger-side wall panel cut for the dimmable light switch](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0445.jpg)

After the panels were finished, we installed the passenger-side upper wall from front to back.

![Completed passenger-side upper wall from front to back](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0446.jpg)

On the driver side, I cut openings in the rear upper wall panel for a pair of reading lights.

![Cutting openings for the driver-side reading lights](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0450.jpg)

We also began adding the plastic trim pieces between the plywood sections to finish the remaining ceiling seams.

![Adding plastic trim between the ceiling panels](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0451.jpg)

Then we installed the adhesive-backed rubber gasket all along the joint where the upper walls meet the ceiling.

![Rubber gasket trim installed along the ceiling and upper wall joint](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0454.jpg)

With the panels and trim in place, we could finally install the first cabinet.

![Installing the first upper cabinet](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0452.jpg)

The reading lights went in next.

![Reading lights installed in the driver-side upper wall](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0453.jpg)

The cabinets hold a surprising amount of gear, and their zippered fronts close easily so nothing can fall out while we are driving.

![Upper cabinet loaded with camping gear](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0456.jpg)

![Upper cabinet zipped closed for travel](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0455.jpg)

From the rear of the van, the finished upper walls and cabinets made the whole interior look far more complete.

![Finished upper walls and cabinets on the driver side](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0457.jpg)

![Finished upper walls and cabinets on the passenger side](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0458.jpg)

After one final inspection, the van was ready for our camping trip.

![Giving the completed upper walls and cabinets a final inspection](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_6685.jpg)

![The van packed and ready for the next camping trip](/images/UpperWall_Cabinets/IMG_0462.jpg)

Upper cabinets were something we had discussed frequently after our first season camping in the van. An empty van has an enormous amount of space, but that space can be surprisingly difficult to organize. Small items always seemed to disappear just when we needed them.

When we took the van out toward the end of July, the new cabinets proved to be exactly what we needed. Our camping equipment—and especially our clothing—was much easier to organize, find, and keep out of the way.
